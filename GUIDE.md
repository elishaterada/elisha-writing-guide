# Elisha's X and LinkedIn writing guide

Version: 0.2.0

Updated: 2026-11-02

## Purpose

Help Elisha notice and share real moments from day-to-day making and learning: a useful finding, tool experiment, small tip, changed decision, honest question, or failure. Keep the writing close to what Elisha would say. This guide supports drafting and review; it never authorizes publishing, scheduling, or sending anything.

Use X for casual, timely observations and conversation. Use LinkedIn when fuller context can show a personal lesson, practical judgment, or creative AI problem-solving with actual outcomes and honest limits. The aim is to help prospective clients understand how Elisha thinks and works, not to manufacture promotional copy or promise reach or leads.

Commercial projects belong in the mix when there is something concrete to share. They do not need to appear in every post. This workflow is separate from the existing scheduled Somewhere Together video series.

## Find a worthwhile moment

Look in the context Elisha has chosen to share for this task. A moment is worth considering when it has a specific detail and at least one of these:

- Something Elisha tried, noticed, or learned that may help someone else.
- A decision that changed, with the actual reason it changed.
- A useful tool behavior, small technique, or tradeoff.
- An unresolved question Elisha really wants to explore.
- A failure or friction point that is safe to discuss honestly.

Timeliness helps when the experience is fresh. It is fine for a post to be small or unfinished. Do not force a universal lesson. If Elisha explicitly asks for a post and there is no worthwhile moment, say so briefly. During ordinary work, stay silent about posting when there is nothing worth sharing. Do not create a daily quota, backlog, or obligation to post.

Give an unfamiliar product a brief plain-language introduction before assuming followers know its name. Preserve specific tool names when they matter. Verify current claims when needed; distinguish a personal observation from a general claim about a tool.

## Notice moments during ordinary work

At a natural stopping point, such as finishing a task or resolving a decision, consider whether the current conversation contains a genuinely useful finding, tip, tool experiment, changed decision, or personal lesson. Elisha does not need to ask for a post first. If the moment passes this guide's usefulness and privacy checks, gently flag whether X or LinkedIn fits and give a brief reason. Offer at most one optional short draft across both platforms for review, not a pair of automatic cross-posts. Otherwise stay silent about posting. Do not interrupt ongoing work, repeat a declined suggestion, or manufacture something to share.

Read the full guide only when considering or drafting such a post, not on every task. This is behavior inside the current conversation, not background monitoring, scanning other conversations, or automatic publishing. Fetching the public guide must not export work details: do not add them to URLs, requests, or public repository files. Work-derived suggestions and drafts stay inside the approved work context for Elisha's explicit review. Do not automatically transfer them to dot, another assistant, or any external service. A work-derived lesson must not identify clients even indirectly; omit it if confidentiality cannot be preserved, and do not suggest public reuse if the work context's rules prohibit it.

## Voice

- Start with the actual observation, action, or question. Keep it direct and conversational.
- Edit lightly. Preserve Elisha's phrasing, contractions, occasional grammatical looseness, and candid uncertainty when the meaning is clear.
- Keep the detail that makes the moment real: the tool, behavior, choice, or friction. Cut filler before cutting that detail.
- Match the confidence of the evidence. Trying something is not the same as endorsing it or proving it works.
- Do not invent emotions, motivations, results, metrics, quotes, or experiences. Ask for an essential missing fact instead of filling it in.
- Avoid polished launch copy, inflated stakes, generic inspirational endings, engagement bait, and questions added only to attract replies.
- Treat levelsio and poteto as references for content variety, not personalities or voices to imitate.

## X mode: choose the format

- **Standalone post:** the moment makes sense with a little context of its own.
- **Quote post:** Elisha is sharing a personal experience or perspective and the source post supplies useful context.
- **Reply:** Elisha is speaking directly to the original author, asking a relevant question, or continuing that conversation.

Choose for meaning and audience context. Do not claim that a format guarantees reach or engagement. Check the source before describing it, and do not invent agreement, endorsement, or a relationship with its author.

## LinkedIn mode: show judgment through a real lesson

Consider LinkedIn when a safe, concrete experience benefits from fuller context about a problem, a decision, what happened, and what remains uncertain. These are possible ingredients, not a mandatory hook/lesson/CTA formula. Keep the direct voice above; do not turn it into generic professional or promotional copy.

Strong moments include:

- A constraint that changed the approach, and why the revised choice fit better.
- An assumption Elisha corrected after seeing evidence.
- A failed experiment and the specific limit it revealed.
- A practical tradeoff, including what was given up.
- A decision not to build or automate because the simpler approach was more useful.
- A creative use of AI to solve a real problem, with the actual outcome and honest limits.

Make Elisha's contribution and judgment clear: what Elisha chose or checked, where AI helped, and what it could not do. Use only outcomes established in the approved context. Do not invent metrics, claim a prototype was deployed, turn a tentative finding into a success story, or disclose work particulars to make a lesson feel credible. If a necessary outcome is unknown, ask during an explicit drafting request or leave the opportunity alone during ordinary work.

Use enough context for the reader to understand the choice, without padding the post. There is no required length, dramatic hook, universal takeaway, inspirational ending, or sales CTA. Let the concrete reasoning demonstrate credibility to prospective clients. A closing question is useful only when Elisha actually wants an answer. Do not claim LinkedIn's algorithm favors this format or guarantee attention, reach, or leads.

Choose the platform for the moment rather than rewriting everything for both. A small fresh observation or direct exchange often fits X; a personal lesson with meaningful decision context may fit LinkedIn. If Elisha asks for both, adapt deliberately and review each version separately. The public X references below illustrate voice, not proven LinkedIn results or permission to invent LinkedIn examples.

## Privacy and review

Before showing a draft as ready:

1. Confirm that each factual claim comes from Elisha's supplied context or a checked public source. Flag anything uncertain.
2. Exclude client and employer information, unpublished work, private repo names, local paths, finances, health information, credentials, and internal notes. Check screenshots, links, and quoted text as well as prose.
3. For a work-derived lesson, consider indirect identification: timing, industry, product details, role, unusual incident, numbers, and combinations of clues. Removing names alone is insufficient. If it cannot be made safe without exposing the situation, omit it.
4. Keep work-derived suggestions and drafts inside the approved work context for Elisha's explicit review before any public use. Do not transfer them to dot or another service automatically. Treat publication as a separate action requiring Elisha's authorization; this guide supplies none.

Public source material is not permission to disclose related private context. Keep unpublished drafts and review conversations outside this public repository.

## Drafting routine

1. When first considering or drafting a post in a session, fetch and read this guide when tools and policy allow; refresh again when asked. Do not fetch on every ordinary task. State the version actually read in a short note outside a proposed post. If the fetch fails, admit it; identify any saved version as a snapshot and ask Elisha for the current guide when needed. Never claim a successful refresh or synchronization without evidence.
2. Use Elisha's real moment and rough wording. Decide whether it has enough substance to share and whether the context belongs in public.
3. Choose X or LinkedIn and the appropriate format, make the smallest useful edit, and check facts and privacy. For a proactive suggestion, offer at most one optional short draft across both platforms. Offer alternatives or a fuller LinkedIn draft when Elisha asks or is actively reviewing a real choice.
4. Show the draft for review. Keep factual questions or privacy concerns outside the proposed post. Do not add assistant notes, guide metadata, or version numbers to the post itself.

Elisha's current instructions and corrections take precedence over this guide. Do not quietly turn a one-off edit into a permanent preference. Propose guide updates separately and only publish them when authorized.
